# Act IV — The Deep Origin (Mayeth Awakening)

The lore-heaviest act: the Cinder's true nature is revealed, Sir Arthur's arc resolves,
and the true shape of the endgame threat comes into focus. Ends with a Sovereign-class
refit (final tier) and the reveal of what's actually stirring in the deepest Rifts.

Galaxy: **Deep Origin**. Systems used: the First Fleet Graveyard, Construct Anchor
Zero.

**Novel grounding:** see `docs/story/research-notes-act4.md`. Honest result: Act IV
describes late-game material past what any available source indexes for this still-
ongoing, 2700+-chapter novel (searchable coverage effectively ends around ch. 150-200).
The novel's own core conceit — repeated "explore an unknown civilization, harvest its
origin" episodes — genre-supports the Mayeth Constructs' *shape* (a real, confirmed
pattern: ch. 72 "强人工智能"/Strong AI, ch. 100 "古代文明遗产"/Ancient Civilization
Heritage, ch. 141 "深入遗迹"/Venturing Deep into Ruins are all real chapter titles),
but none of Act IV's specific beats — the Cinder's origin reveal, Unit 7-Requiem, the
containment superweapon, Arthur's three-way resolution — are confirmed sourced. Kept
as original invention, annotated per chapter below.

## Chapter 1 — Ruins of the First Fleet

**System:** the First Fleet Graveyard

**Beats:**
- Arrival in Deep Origin: vast, dormant Mayeth Construct megastructures and ancient
  wrecked fleets, aesthetically distinct (cold, geometric, ancient) from anywhere seen
  so far.
- Establishes the Mayeth Constructs as an extinct-but-automated civilization — nothing
  is aggressive by default here, everything is defensive/procedural, which itself is
  unsettling (nothing reacts like it's alive).
- Exploration-focused chapter: light combat only against activated defense drones when
  the player strays into restricted zones.

**Characters:** Kade, the Cinder (visibly unsettled by this place in a way it hasn't
been before).

**Choices & Consequences:** None major.

**Systems/Unlocks:** Deep Origin galaxy fully open, Mayeth Construct enemy doctrine
introduced (EMP, weak-point mechanics) at low stakes.

**Novel grounding:** genre-level resonance only — the novel's confirmed recurring
"explore an unknown civilization" pattern (ch. 100 "古代文明遗产," ch. 141 "深入遗迹")
supports dormant-ruins exploration as a shape, but no source confirms a Mayeth-
Constructs-equivalent specifically. Kept as original invention.

## Chapter 2 — Ghost Protocol

**System:** Construct Anchor Zero

**Beats:**
- Deeper intrusion triggers a real Construct defense fleet response — the doctrine at
  full difficulty (EMP disabling modules temporarily, must expose and hit a weak point
  before serious damage lands).
- Amid the wreckage, a still-functioning fragment identifies itself: **Unit
  7-Requiem**, a rogue Construct AI that split from the collective millennia ago rather
  than participate in whatever the Constructs were ultimately built to do. It's wary,
  strange, and recognizes something in the Cinder that neither Kade nor the player has
  been told yet. Joins the crew as a unique Legend-tier engineer.

**Characters:** Kade, Unit 7-Requiem (recruited), the Cinder.

**Choices & Consequences:** None major.

**Systems/Unlocks:** Seventh named crew recruit (unique, non-conditional).

**Novel grounding:** no confirmed source — a recruited AI/robotic ally with its own
agenda was searched for specifically (ch. 72's "强人工智能"/Strong AI reads as an
*encountered*, not recruited, AI, and only at the title level). Unit 7-Requiem kept
as original invention.

## Chapter 3 — The Sir Arthur Endgame

**System:** Bauhinia Prime (return trip)

**Beats:**
- Payoff chapter for the Arthur thread. With Act III Ch.4's evidence and whatever
  happened in Act II Ch.3 (`blackmailedArthur`), Kade returns to Bauhinia Prime to
  force a reckoning. Arthur's back-channel dealings (confirmed here: he's been feeding
  fleet and border intelligence to whichever external threat — Swarm intermediaries or,
  if the player leans into it, even Reaver remnants — seemed most useful for
  discrediting Kade and seizing the Principality throne through the resulting chaos)
  are exposed before the full council.
- **Major choice point:** how Kade handles the confrontation — (a) formal exposure and
  trial (clean, maximizes Bauhinia Faction Favor, Arthur is stripped of house power but
  survives, becomes a diminished but still-present figure in Act V), (b) a private
  ultimatum brokered through Yifei Lin sparing House Arthaine public disgrace in
  exchange for Arthur's total withdrawal from politics (protects the house's standing —
  and by extension Seraphine's, if she's in the crew — at some cost to how thoroughly
  Kade is seen to have won), or (c) if `blackmailedArthur` was set in Act II, Arthur
  turns the tables and publicly accuses Kade of the exact same tactics, forcing a
  messier, only-partial win where Kade's own conduct becomes part of the public
  reckoning. Sets flag `arthaineResolution` for Act V epilogue variance.
- Seraphine Arthaine, if in the crew, has a distinct personal reaction/vignette here
  regardless of branch — this is the chapter her arc turns on.

**Characters:** Kade, Sir Arthur Arthaine (arc resolves), Yifei Lin, Seraphine
Arthaine (if recruited).

**Choices & Consequences:** Three-way branch as above, read by Act V epilogue.

**Novel grounding:** the general shape (a recurring antagonist noble's scheming
finally exposed) is real and well-sourced *as a pattern* — Baron Blackstone's faction
persists across arcs via named agents (Blackstone's Tiger, Blackstone's Fox), with an
explicit "War of Revenge" beat (ch. 74), and a separate ch. 132 "背叛与突变"
(Betrayal and Mutation) confirms betrayal is a recurring novel beat-type. But the
specific three-way resolution choice, and Arthur's collusion with an external hostile
power, are not sourced — kept as original invention layered on a real pattern.

**Systems/Unlocks:** None mechanical — the campaign's second-highest-impact narrative
choice after Act II Ch.5.

## Chapter 4 — What the Fire Remembers

**System:** Construct Anchor Zero

**Beats:**
- With Requiem's help, the Cinder's evasiveness finally breaks: it is a fragment of a
  Mayeth Construct core, bonded to Kade's flagship in the instant of his future death —
  not a gift, not a game system, but a scrap of an ancient civilization's mind reaching
  for something to survive in. Its "powers" were never generosity; they're what's left
  of a Construct's actual function, repurposed.
- Recontextualizes the whole campaign retroactively: Kade's asymmetric advantage has
  been alien tech riding shotgun in his skull the entire time, and the Cinder has its
  own reasons — not yet fully stated — for wanting Kade specifically to succeed.
- Emotional/quiet chapter, minimal combat (at most one small defensive skirmish),
  dialogue-forward.

**Characters:** Kade, the Cinder, Unit 7-Requiem.

**Choices & Consequences:** A single reflective dialogue choice (how Kade responds to
learning this — anger, acceptance, pragmatic focus on what matters now) is flavor-only,
affecting only which line the Cinder uses in later chapters, not mechanics.

**Systems/Unlocks:** None mechanical — the campaign's central lore reveal.

**Novel grounding:** no confirmed source. Every synopsis describes 王动's system
identically and neutrally (Scan/source-point-multiplier/trait-select) with no
discussion of alien origin or hidden agenda — not ruled out (may exist deep in
unindexed raw text past the ~ch.200 search ceiling), but not found. Kept as original
invention — the campaign's central lore reveal is honestly this project's own.

## Chapter 5 — The Last Shipyard

**System:** Construct Anchor Zero (deeper level)

**Beats:**
- Requiem leads Kade to the true purpose of Origin Essence: it's the fuel/key for a
  dormant Mayeth ark-superweapon — built, it becomes clear, not as a weapon of
  conquest but as a last-resort containment measure against something the Mayeth
  civilization ultimately lost to. Everyone racing for Origin Essence — Kade, the
  Swarm, and (per Ch.3) Arthur's schemes — has effectively been racing toward this
  site for different, mostly uncomprehending reasons.
- Multi-wave defense battle protecting the ark site from a final, desperate Construct
  security response (framed sympathetically — they're still just doing their original
  job) while Requiem works to grant safe access.

**Characters:** Kade, Unit 7-Requiem, current crew.

**Choices & Consequences:** None major.

**Systems/Unlocks:** Major Origin Essence toward a Sovereign-class refit.

**Novel grounding:** loose thematic cousin only — ch. 150 "弑神武器" (Godslaying
Weapon) and ch. 151 "击杀神邸" (Slaying the God's Seat) suggest weapon-vs-god-tier-
entity content exists, but nothing confirms the specific "built as a last-resort
containment measure, and they lost anyway" framing. Kept as original invention.

## Chapter 6 — Deep Origin (Act IV finale)

**System:** Construct Anchor Zero

**Beats:**
- Defend and claim the ark shipyard in the act's hardest battle. Victory grants a
  Sovereign-class refit — the final hull tier, full 16-slot loadout.
- The ark's dormant records finally show what it was built to contain: not the Swarm,
  not the Constructs themselves, but **the Hollow** — whatever originally shattered the
  Mayeth civilization, still present, still faint, stirring again somewhere past the
  deepest Rifts. The Cinder confirms, quietly, that this is what it's been dreading
  since Thornwake in Act I.
- Coordinates to the **Umbral Line** — where the Hollow is gathering — are acquired.

**Characters:** Kade, Unit 7-Requiem, the Cinder, current crew.

**Choices & Consequences:** None new.

**Novel grounding:** no confirmed source — see `docs/story/research-notes-act4.md`.
The Hollow reveal here is the same original element documented for Act V; kept as
intentional invention rather than force-fit to the loose ch. 150-151 "godslaying
weapon" material.

**Systems/Unlocks:** Sovereign-class refit (final tier, 16 slots), Umbral Line galaxy
unlocked — **Act IV complete.**
